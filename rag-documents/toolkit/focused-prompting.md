# One Prompt, One Job

## The Idea

When you ask an AI to do ten things at once, you get ten shallow results.
When you ask it to do one thing, you get one deep result.

This is not a hard rule. It is a reliable pattern.

## Why It Happens

AI models read the *shape* of your request before they respond. A prompt with ten tasks looks like a checklist, so the model responds in checklist mode: brief, surface-level, one line per item.

A prompt with a single focused task looks like it deserves serious attention, so the model gives it that. More reasoning, more nuance, more useful output.

It is less about the AI running out of space and more about it calibrating depth to match what you appear to want.

## What This Looks Like in Practice

**Instead of this:**

> Summarise the report, identify the key risks, suggest three recommendations, rewrite the executive summary, and check the tone.

**Do this:**

> Summarise the report in three to four sentences, focusing on the main argument.

Then, in the next message:

> Now identify the key risks mentioned.

And so on.

Each message gets the AI's full attention. Each response is noticeably better.

## The Underlying Principle

Think of working with AI as a conversation, not a task list handed to an assistant on their way out the door. In a good conversation, you deal with one thing at a time. You follow up. You build.

Splitting your request across ten items is a shortcut that costs you quality. Taking it one step at a time is slower in theory, but faster in practice because you spend less time fixing weak output.

## A Simple Test

Next time you have a complex task, try it both ways. Ask everything at once, then start a fresh session and work through it one prompt at a time. The difference in depth and usefulness is usually obvious.
